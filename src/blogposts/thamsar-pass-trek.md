# Thamsar Pass: A 45km, Two-Day Race Against Time Through Glaciers and Rainbows

There's a particular kind of madness in deciding to squeeze a 43-45km high-altitude trek into a single weekend. But that's exactly what my friend Dheeraj and I did — and it turned out to be one of the most memorable treks of my life.

## The Plan That Almost Didn't Happen

It was June, and Dheeraj and I were both back home in Joginder Nagar. Being on WFH meant I only had two days to spare — leave home Saturday morning, be back by Sunday evening. Dheeraj suggested Thamsar Pass, and that was that. No overthinking, just a plan.

We were originally supposed to be a group of four. Then one person backed out the day before, and a third wasn't answering calls. Dheeraj and I decided we weren't going to let that stop us — if it came down to it, the two of us would go alone. We couldn't skip this trek.

Then, almost on cue, just as we reached Billing — about an hour from home — the third person called to say he was coming after all, and had just left his house. So we waited. And waited. Over an hour passed at Billing with no sign of him.

![View from Billing while waiting](1_view_from_billing.jpg)
*Killing time at Billing, waiting for our third companion who was still an hour away.*

Eventually we gave up waiting and pushed on to Rajgundha, starting the actual trek at around 10:30 AM — just the two of us.

## Day 1: Rajgundha to Panahartu Campsite

### A Wrong Turn and an Unexpected Reunion

We hadn't gone far before realizing we'd taken the wrong route. A short, unplanned ascent later, we were back on track — and to our surprise, we ran into our third friend, who had also started the trek on his own. Confused for a second, then genuinely happy, the three of us continued together.

![Starting the trek from Rajgundha](2_starting_from_rajgundha.jpg)
*The trek begins — Rajgundha behind us, Thamsar Pass somewhere far ahead.*

### Palachak and the Best Maggi Break

We reached Palachak around 11:45 AM, not particularly tired but glad for a break anyway. There's a small dukan (shop) here, and — as any Himalayan trekker will tell you — a plate of maggi and omelette at altitude tastes better than any five-star meal. We rested, ate, and got moving again by 12:30 PM.

![Reached Palachak](3_reached_palachak.jpg)
*Palachak — home to the last proper meal stop before the trail gets serious.*

### Waterfalls, Old Huts, and a Trail of Bones

The weather was kind and the scenery kept getting better. Soon we came across a beautiful waterfall next to an old stone hut — likely used by shepherds who regularly travel this route between the main town and the remote village of Bhara Bhangal. We stopped for about ten minutes just to take it in.

![Waterfall after Palachak](4_waterfall_after_palachak.jpg)
*A stunning waterfall along the trail, next to a shepherd's hut.*

![Rocky trail after the waterfall](5_rocky_trail_after_waterfall.jpg)
*The trail turns rocky as we climb higher.*

A little further along, we found something unsettling — multiple bones scattered along the path, likely belonging to horses and sheep that shepherds bring with them on their seasonal migrations. Our guess: wild animals had gotten to them. A sobering reminder of how wild this route really is.

![Bones found along the trail](6_multiple_bones_after_waterfaill.jpg)
*Animal bones along the trail — a stark reminder of the terrain's wildness.*

### Rain, Raincoats, and Slower Progress

Not long after, the rain started. Raincoats went on, and with them, our pace dropped — walking in a raincoat on uneven terrain is never fun. Still, we pushed through.

![It started raining](7_started_raining.jpg)
*The weather turns — raincoats on, pace down, spirits still up.*

## The Glacier Crossing: The Real Challenge

As we climbed, snow-capped mountains and glaciers came into view for the first time. From a distance, we assumed the relatively flat glaciers ahead — where we could see other trekkers crossing — were what we'd have to deal with. We were wrong. Between us and that flat stretch stood a steep glacier we hadn't accounted for.

![First glimpse of snow mountains and glaciers](8_started_seeing_snow_mountain_and_glacier_crossing_view.jpg)
*Our first view of the snow mountains — deceptively far from what we'd actually have to cross.*

There was no clear route around it. With no better option, we decided to cross the steep glacier directly — digging a foothold with each step, testing it, then digging the next one, careful not to slip. It was slow, tense, and genuinely risky.

![Crossing the steep glacier](9_actual_glacier_crossing.jpg)
*Digging footholds step by step across the steep glacier — one of the trek's most intense moments.*

After clearing that one, we crossed two more glaciers. These were less steep and easier to manage, but still demanded full attention with every step.

## Reaching Panahartu Campsite

By the time we crossed the last glacier, it was around 6 PM, and we reached our campsite at Panahartu — tired but relieved. We pitched our tent immediately.

![Reached Panahartu campsite](10_reached_panahart_campsite_vieew.jpg)
*Panahartu campsite — our home for the night, at the end of a long first day.*

We weren't alone — a group of five trekkers with a guide had also set up camp and were cooking dinner. Too exhausted to cook for ourselves, we struck a deal: dinner for around ₹150 per person. We ate, talked about the plan for the next day, and turned in early.

![Campsite at night](11_campsite_night_view.jpg)
*A quiet, misty night at the campsite — a rare still moment on the trek.*

## Day 2: The Push to Thamsar Pass

We woke up around 6 AM. The other group had already left at 5 AM to reach the pass, but we decided to start a little later — confident we could cover the distance faster. A quick breakfast of maggi and chai, and we set off by 7 AM.

![Morning view from the campsite](12_campsite_morning_view.jpg)
*Morning at Panahartu — clear skies before the day's climb.*

### Bhedpal: The Campsite We Wish We'd Known About

We reached Bhedpal soon after, and it turned out to be stunning — arguably a better campsite than Panahartu. In hindsight, we wished we'd pushed a little further on Day 1 to camp here instead. We rested, took some photos, and pressed on.

![Reached Bhedpal](13_reached_bhedpal.jpg)
*Bhedpal — a beautiful spot we didn't know existed until we got there.*

![Resting after Bhedpal](14_rest_after_bhedpal.jpg)
*A short breather after Bhedpal before the steeper terrain ahead.*

### The Final Climb to the Pass

The trail beyond Bhedpal was noticeably steeper and tougher than anything from the previous day, so we took another break to recover. Eventually, we reached a plain, elevated stretch where we got our first glimpse of Thamsar Lake — still blanketed in snow, its actual boundaries impossible to make out. We chose not to walk right up to it, unsure of where solid ground ended.

What followed was a ridge walk that ranks among the most beautiful stretches of the entire trek.

![The last stretch along the snowline](15_last_snowline_stretch.jpg)
*The snowbound stretch leading toward Thamsar Lake.*

After the ridge, one last obstacle stood between us and the pass: a 300-400m steep climb at an elevation of roughly 4,500-4,600m. The thin air made every step harder than it should have been, and this section ate up a good chunk of time.

![Thamsar Lake, snow-covered](16_thamsar_lake_view.jpg)
*Thamsar Lake, still covered in snow with no clear shoreline visible.*

## The Pass

We finally reached Thamsar Pass at around 9:30-9:45 AM. The view was, in a word, mesmerizing — Thamsar Lake below, mountain ranges all around, and clouds drifting through the whole scene. There's a small temple at the pass too, built by shepherds over the years as a place to pray for safe passage on their journeys between Bhara Bhangal and Rajgundha.

![View from the top of the pass](17_lake_view_from_pass_top.jpg)
*The view from the top — Thamsar Lake and the surrounding peaks laid out below.*

![At Thamsar Pass](18_thamsar_pass.jpg)
*At Thamsar Pass — the goal we'd been climbing toward since Saturday morning.*

We took our photos, soaked in the moment, and started the descent.

### A Rainbow to Remember

As if the trek hadn't already given us enough, we were treated to a rainbow arching between the snow-covered peaks during the descent — one of those sights that's hard to describe and even harder to forget.

![Rainbow during the descent](19_rainbow_during_decent_from_pass.jpg)
*A rainbow between the snow mountains during our descent — pure luck, pure beauty.*

## The Long Way Back

We packed up our tent, shouldered our backpacks, and began the return journey to Rajgundha, finally arriving around 5-6 PM — thoroughly exhausted but deeply satisfied.

![Heading back down](20_going_back.jpg)
*On the way back — tired legs, full hearts.*

## The Numbers

- **Total distance:** ~43-45 km (21-22 km one way)
- **Duration:** 2 days, 1 night
- **Toughest stretch:** The final 3-3.5 km climb from Panahartu campsite to the pass — took us 2-2.5 hours (7:00 AM to 9:30/9:45 AM) at 4,500-4,600m elevation
- **Route:** Rajgundha → Palachak → Waterfall → Glacier crossings → Panahartu campsite → Bhedpal → Thamsar Lake → Thamsar Pass

## Final Thoughts

Thamsar Pass gave us everything a trek should — uncertainty at the start, a genuinely risky glacier crossing, exhaustion, warm food shared with strangers, and a summit view that made every step worth it. Doing it in just two days was intense, and there are things we'd do differently next time — mainly, camping at Bhedpal instead of Panahartu to make the final push a little easier.

But if given the choice, I'd do it again in a heartbeat. And next time, the plan is to go all the way to Bhara Bhangal.
